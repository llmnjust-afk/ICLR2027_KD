\begin{abstract}
Dataset distillation seeks to compress large-scale datasets into compact synthetic surrogates that preserve training utility. Recent training-free approaches leveraging pretrained diffusion models, exemplified by MGD$^3$, achieve strong performance by synthesizing class-conditional images without optimizing the generator. However, these methods apply a globally fixed classifier-free guidance (CFG) strength across all classes and timesteps, ignoring that different classes exhibit varying structural complexity and that the denoising process traverses fundamentally different regimes at different noise levels. We propose \ags{} (Adaptive Guidance Scheduling for Diffusion Dataset Distillation), a training-free framework with three complementary modules: \cags{} (Class-Adaptive Guidance Strength), which assigns per-class guidance based on a multi-factor complexity metric combining mode count, intra-class variance, and inter-class separability; \tags{} (Timestep-Adaptive Guidance Schedule), which modulates guidance across diffusion timesteps; and \iast{} (IPC-Adaptive Stop Timing), which adjusts the generation window based on the images-per-class budget. All modules operate at inference time on a frozen pretrained DiT, adding zero trainable parameters. Experiments on ImageNet-100, ImageNette, and ImageWoof demonstrate that \cags{} yields consistent improvements: $+29.3\%$ relative on 100-class ImageNet-100 (IPC=10) and $+23.3\%$ at IPC=50, $+23.0\%$ on ImageNette, and $+20.8\%$ on ImageWoof over the unguided baseline, while fixed guidance either marginally helps or actively hurts depending on the dataset. Combining \cags{} with \tags{} further improves performance to $+31.9\%$ relative on 100-class ImageNet-100, confirming that per-class and per-timestep adaptation are complementary.
\end{abstract}
