\begin{abstract}
Dataset distillation seeks to compress large-scale datasets into compact synthetic surrogates that preserve training utility. Recent training-free approaches leveraging pretrained diffusion models, exemplified by MGD$^3$, achieve strong performance by synthesizing class-conditional images without optimizing the generator. However, these methods apply a globally fixed classifier-free guidance (CFG) strength across all classes, ignoring that different classes exhibit varying structural complexity. We propose \ags{} (Adaptive Guidance Scheduling for Diffusion Dataset Distillation), a training-free framework whose core module, \cags{} (Class-Adaptive Guidance Strength), assigns per-class guidance based on a multi-factor complexity metric. Through systematic factor ablation, we find that per-class adaptation---not merely the presence of guidance---drives the improvement: intra-class variance, which produces the widest per-class guidance variation, is the most effective single factor, matching the full multi-factor metric, while inter-class separability, which produces near-constant guidance, is the least effective. Two complementary modules address secondary axes of variation: \iast{} (IPC-Adaptive Guidance Range) scales the guidance range as a function of the images-per-class budget, and \tags{} (Timestep-Adaptive Guidance Schedule) modulates guidance across diffusion timesteps; both are investigated but ultimately not recommended based on negative ablation results. All modules operate at inference time on a frozen pretrained DiT, adding zero trainable parameters. Experiments on ImageNet-100, ImageNette, and ImageWoof across ConvNet-6, ResNet-18, and ResNetAP-10 demonstrate that \cags{} yields consistent and substantial improvements on the 100-class subset: $+7.6\%$ relative at IPC=10 and $+14.8\%$ at IPC=50, with gains scaling on deeper architectures ($+17.0\%$ on ResNet-18, $+13.4\%$ on ResNetAP-10). On the 10-class subset, \cags{} provides a large gain at IPC=50 ($+9.0\%$) but is neutral at IPC=10 and \emph{harmful} at IPC=1 ($-12.8\%$), revealing a boundary condition: per-class adaptation requires sufficient class complexity variation. On ImageWoof, where all classes share similar structural complexity, \cags{} \emph{hurts} ($-5.2\%$), further confirming this boundary. Critically, fixed guidance \emph{hurts} on the 100-class subset ($-1.5\%$ relative), whereas \cags{} provides consistent gains, confirming that per-class adaptation---not merely the presence of guidance---drives the improvement. Statistical significance is confirmed via paired $t$-tests across three seeds.
\end{abstract}
