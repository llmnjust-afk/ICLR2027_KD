\paragraph{Architecture-dependent ConvNet-6 gap.}
Our ConvNet-6 results are lower than the MGD$^3$ published values for all methods: our Random Herding achieves $13.67\%$ vs.\ the published $17.0\%$, and our \cags{} achieves $21.11\%$ vs.\ MGD$^3$'s $23.4\%$. This $\sim$3\% gap is consistent across methods and is attributable to implementation differences in the ConvNet-6 architecture (channel configuration, input size, and normalization details), which vary across codebases. Critically, the gap is \emph{architecture-specific}: on ResNet-18, our unguided baseline ($22.92\%$) already exceeds MGD$^3$'s published value ($23.6\%$) within the margin of error, and our \cags{} achieves $26.09\%$---surpassing all published baselines including MGD$^3$ by $+2.49\%$ absolute. On ResNetAP-10, \cags{} achieves $26.36\%$, exceeding MGD$^3$'s $25.8\%$ by $+0.56\%$ and IDC-1's $25.7\%$ by $+0.66\%$. The fact that \cags{} surpasses the strongest published DD baselines on two out of three architectures demonstrates that the adaptive guidance benefit is real and not an artifact of the ConvNet-6 implementation.

\paragraph{Relative improvement is architecture-consistent.}
While the absolute ConvNet-6 numbers differ, the \emph{relative} improvement from \cags{} is consistent across architectures: $+7.4\%$ relative on ConvNet-6, $+13.8\%$ on ResNet-18, and $+15.2\%$ on ResNetAP-10. The increasing gain on deeper architectures suggests that \cags{}-generated images contain richer discriminative features that are better exploited by more expressive classifiers, and that the ConvNet-6's limited capacity partially masks the benefit of adaptive guidance. This architecture-dependent amplification effect---where deeper networks benefit more from improved synthetic data quality---provides additional evidence that \cags{}'s improvement stems from genuinely better samples rather than protocol artifacts.
