\subsection{Scaling Analysis: From 10 to 1000 Classes}
\label{sec:scaling-200}

A central claim of \cags{} is that per-class adaptive guidance becomes increasingly beneficial as the number of classes grows. We evaluate \cags{} across class counts from 10 to 1000, keeping all other settings fixed (IPC=10, instance normalization). The full ImageNet-1K~\cite{russakovsky2014imagenet} evaluation uses all 1000 classes with 1000 training epochs (reduced from 2000 due to the 10$\times$ scale).

\Cref{tab:scaling} presents the scaling results. The \cags{} advantage over unguided grows substantially: $+1.19\%$ absolute on 44 classes, $+2.91\%$ on 100 classes, $+3.30\%$ on 200 classes, and $+3.58\%$ on 1000 classes (ConvNet-6). On ImageNet-1K, the advantage amplifies on deeper architectures: $+6.97\%$ ($+41.3\%$ relative) on ResNet-18 and $+6.83\%$ ($+43.7\%$ relative) on ResNetAP-10. This three-architecture scaling result provides the strongest evidence for the scaling hypothesis: the benefit of per-class adaptive guidance grows with both class count and classifier capacity, consistent with the adaptivity gap theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptivity-gap}).
