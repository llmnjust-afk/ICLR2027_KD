\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The two-factor configuration $(\alpha{=}0, \beta{=}0.5, \gamma{=}0.5, \delta{=}0)$ is the recommended default; the four-factor design may be preferred on datasets with high mode-count or separability variation (Appendix~\ref{sec:semantic-subset}). While our main 100-class results use five seeds, some supplementary experiments use only three. We follow MGD$^3$'s best-epoch tracking protocol, reporting peak top-1 accuracy across all training epochs; this may overestimate practical performance versus final-epoch evaluation, though it ensures fair comparison with prior work. The theoretical analysis assumes well-separated Gaussian modes and a second-order approximation; the linear-in-$\lambda$ benefit term is valid only for small $\lambda$ ($\lambda \in [0, 0.06]$). Non-ImageNet results use SD~1.5, yielding low absolute accuracy on Food-101 ($4.17\%$) that reflects the generator's domain gap rather than a \cags{} limitation. The 10-class scaling boundary depends on cross-class complexity variation (Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptivity-gap}), not class count alone: ImageNette (10 classes, $+5.28\%$) shows substantial gains, while the 10-class ImageNet-100 subset shows $0\%$ gain due to narrow variation. All hyperparameters are in Section~\ref{sec:setup}; code will be released upon acceptance.
