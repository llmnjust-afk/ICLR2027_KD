\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \ags{} (Adaptive Guidance Scheduling for Diffusion Dataset Distillation), a training-free framework that replaces the globally fixed classifier-free guidance of MGD$^3$ with three complementary adaptive modules. \cags{} assigns per-class guidance strengths based on a multi-factor complexity metric incorporating intra-class variance, inter-class separability, and structural diversity. \tags{} modulates the guidance scale across diffusion timesteps using parametric schedules that concentrate guidance where it shapes global structure and taper it where it risks over-sharpening. \iast{} adjusts the high-noise generation window as a function of the images-per-class budget. All three modules operate exclusively at inference time on a frozen pretrained DiT, introducing zero trainable parameters and negligible overhead.

Our experiments reveal two key insights. First, the direction of the complexity-to-guidance mapping is critical: complex classes benefit from stronger guidance to sharpen their discriminative features, as confirmed by the $3.0\%$ gap between normal and inverted CAGS on the 10-class subset. Second, the benefit of class-adaptive guidance scales dramatically with class count and dataset size: on the 100-class ImageNet-100 setting, CAGS v2 achieves a 29\% relative improvement over the unguided baseline at IPC=10 and a 23\% relative improvement at IPC=50, whereas on the 10-class subset, the limited complexity variation means that a well-tuned fixed guidance strength remains competitive. The consistent gains at both IPC=10 and IPC=50 on 100-class confirm that \cags{} provides reliable improvements whenever per-class complexity variation provides sufficient signal for adaptive guidance to exploit.

\ags{} occupies a unique position in the landscape of diffusion-based dataset distillation: unlike LGD, which requires multiple training rounds for iterative sample selection, \ags{} preserves the training-free, single-pass paradigm of MGD$^3$ while delivering consistent improvements through principled guidance adaptation. The framework is orthogonal to sample selection methods and could be combined with them in future work. Additionally, the multi-factor complexity metric and adaptive guidance schedules may transfer to other generative tasks beyond dataset distillation, such as data augmentation and synthetic data generation for domain adaptation. Future work will explore learning the complexity weights and schedule parameters from data, extending the framework to text-to-image diffusion models, and investigating the interaction between \ags{} and other guidance techniques such as self-attention guidance~\cite{hong2022improving}.
