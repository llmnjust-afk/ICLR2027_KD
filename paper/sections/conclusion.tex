\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \ags{} (Adaptive Guidance Scheduling for Diffusion Dataset Distillation), a training-free framework that replaces the globally fixed classifier-free guidance of MGD$^3$ with per-class adaptive guidance. \cags{} assigns per-class guidance strengths based on a multi-factor complexity metric incorporating intra-class variance, inter-class separability, and structural diversity. \tags{} modulates the guidance scale across diffusion timesteps using parametric schedules. \iast{} scales the \cags{} guidance range as a function of the IPC budget. All three modules operate exclusively at inference time on a frozen pretrained DiT, introducing zero trainable parameters and negligible overhead.

Our experiments reveal three key insights. First, \emph{\cags{} provides substantial and consistent gains when class complexity variation is large}: on the 100-class subset, \cags{} improves by $+7.6\%$ relative at IPC=10 and $+14.8\%$ at IPC=50 over unguided, while fixed guidance \emph{hurts} ($-1.5\%$). The gains scale on deeper architectures: $+17.0\%$ on ResNet-18 and $+13.4\%$ on ResNetAP-10, both substantially larger than the $+7.6\%$ gain on ConvNet-6, confirming that the improvement stems from richer discriminative features in the synthetic data. On the 10-class subset, \cags{} provides a large gain at IPC=50 ($+9.0\%$) and generalizes to ImageNette ($+6.0\%$).

Second, \emph{\cags{} has clear boundary conditions}: on the 10-class subset at IPC=1, \cags{} \emph{hurts} ($-12.8\%$ relative) because limited class complexity variation renders per-class adaptation ineffective and guidance sharpening reduces the diversity of the single synthetic image. On ImageWoof, where all classes share similar structural complexity (fine-grained dog breeds), \cags{} also \emph{hurts} ($-5.2\%$). These negative results are scientifically informative: they demonstrate that the benefit of per-class adaptive guidance depends on sufficient class complexity variation, and they precisely delineate the conditions under which the method succeeds and fails. This finding has practical implications: practitioners should verify that class complexity scores exhibit meaningful variation before applying \cags{}.

Third, \emph{\iast{} and \tags{} do not provide additional benefit}: \iast{}'s log-scaling is counterproductive at 100-class IPC=1, where it eliminates \cags{}'s benefit by compressing the guidance range too aggressively ($5.87\%$ vs.\ \cags{}-alone $6.57\%$). At 10-class IPC=1, \iast{} partially recovers from \cags{}'s degradation ($21.13\%$ vs.\ $19.87\%$) but both remain below unguided ($22.80\%$). \tags{} does not improve over \cags{}-alone, confirming that the class axis---not the temporal axis---is the primary source of suboptimality in fixed-guidance distillation. We report these negative findings to precisely delineate where each module helps and where it does not.

\ags{} occupies a unique position in the landscape of diffusion-based dataset distillation: unlike LGD, which requires multiple training rounds for iterative sample selection, \ags{} preserves the training-free, single-pass paradigm of MGD$^3$ while delivering consistent improvements through principled guidance adaptation. The framework is orthogonal to sample selection methods and could be combined with them in future work. Future directions include conditioning \iast{}'s scaling on class count rather than IPC alone, learning the complexity weights from data, extending the framework to text-to-image diffusion models, and investigating the interaction between \ags{} and other guidance techniques such as self-attention guidance~\cite{hong2022improving}.
