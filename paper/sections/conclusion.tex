\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \ags{} (Adaptive Guidance Scheduling for Diffusion Dataset Distillation), a training-free framework that replaces the globally fixed classifier-free guidance of MGD$^3$ with three complementary adaptive modules. \cags{} assigns per-class guidance strengths based on a multi-factor complexity metric incorporating intra-class variance, inter-class separability, and structural diversity. \tags{} modulates the guidance scale across diffusion timesteps using parametric schedules that concentrate guidance where it shapes global structure and taper it where it risks over-sharpening. \iast{} scales the \cags{} guidance range as a function of the IPC budget, protecting low-budget settings from over-aggressive per-class variation. All three modules operate exclusively at inference time on a frozen pretrained DiT, introducing zero trainable parameters and negligible overhead.

Our experiments reveal three key insights. First, fixed classifier-free guidance can actively harm performance when class complexity varies significantly: on the 100-class ImageNet-100 subset, a globally fixed $\lambda = 0.10$ \emph{decreases} accuracy by $5.2\%$ relative to the unguided baseline, while on the 10-class subset, a well-tuned fixed $\lambda$ provides only marginal improvement. This asymmetry underscores the fundamental limitation of globally fixed guidance: no single $\lambda$ is optimal across classes with heterogeneous complexity. Second, \cags{} resolves this limitation by assigning per-class guidance strengths proportional to class complexity, yielding consistent improvements across all IPC settings on the 10-class subset ($+4.6\%$ to $+18.1\%$ relative) and overcoming the failure of fixed guidance on the 100-class subset ($+3.3\%$ relative over unguided, $+8.9\%$ over fixed). The gains are architecture-agnostic, with \cags{} yielding $+20.8\%$ relative on ResNet-18 and $+19.9\%$ on ResNetAP-10, confirming that the improvement stems from the synthetic data rather than architecture-specific bias. Third, \tags{} and \iast{} each address a distinct axis of variation: \tags{} provides temporal smoothing that further improves 100-class accuracy to $+9.0\%$ over unguided with the tightest variance, while \iast{} protects IPC=1 settings where full-range \cags{} is harmful, recovering from $-8.0\%$ to $-4.9\%$ on 100 classes and boosting 10-class from $+11.8\%$ to $+18.1\%$.

\ags{} occupies a unique position in the landscape of diffusion-based dataset distillation: unlike LGD, which requires multiple training rounds for iterative sample selection, \ags{} preserves the training-free, single-pass paradigm of MGD$^3$ while delivering consistent improvements through principled guidance adaptation. The framework is orthogonal to sample selection methods and could be combined with them in future work. Additionally, the multi-factor complexity metric and adaptive guidance schedules may transfer to other generative tasks beyond dataset distillation, such as data augmentation and synthetic data generation for domain adaptation. Future work will explore learning the complexity weights and schedule parameters from data, extending the framework to text-to-image diffusion models, and investigating the interaction between \ags{} and other guidance techniques such as self-attention guidance~\cite{hong2022improving}.
