\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \ags{} (Adaptive Guidance Scheduling for Diffusion Dataset Distillation), a training-free framework that replaces the globally fixed classifier-free guidance of MGD$^3$ with three complementary adaptive modules. \cags{} assigns per-class guidance strengths based on a multi-factor complexity metric incorporating intra-class variance, inter-class separability, and structural diversity. \tags{} modulates the guidance scale across diffusion timesteps using parametric schedules that concentrate guidance where it shapes global structure and taper it where it risks over-sharpening. \iast{} scales the \cags{} guidance range as a function of the IPC budget, protecting low-budget settings from over-aggressive per-class variation. All three modules operate exclusively at inference time on a frozen pretrained DiT, introducing zero trainable parameters and negligible overhead.

Our experiments reveal three key insights. First, \emph{\cags{} consistently outperforms both unguided and fixed guidance across all tested settings}: on the 10-class subset, \cags{} surpasses the best fixed $\lambda$ ($50.13\%$ vs.\ $48.47\%$ at IPC=10), demonstrating that per-class adaptation is beneficial even with limited class count; on the 100-class subset, fixed guidance \emph{hurts} ($-1.9\%$ relative) while \cags{} improves by $+7.4\%$ over unguided and $+9.4\%$ over fixed guidance. The gain increases monotonically with IPC, reaching $+19.4\%$ relative at 100-class IPC=50, and generalizes to ImageNette ($+16.8\%$) and ImageWoof ($+13.7\%$). The improvements are architecture-agnostic, with \cags{} yielding $+13.8\%$ relative on ResNet-18 and $+15.2\%$ on ResNetAP-10, both substantially larger than the $+7.4\%$ gain on ConvNet-6, confirming that the improvement stems from the synthetic data rather than architecture-specific bias.

Second, \emph{\iast{} provides essential protection for the hardest setting but reveals an important boundary condition}: at 100-class IPC=1, full-range \cags{} barely improves over unguided ($+1.6\%$ relative), but \iast{}-scaled \cags{} achieves $+11.2\%$---the best result at this setting. However, the same log-scaling \emph{hurts} on 10-class IPC=1 ($-13.0\%$ relative), because aggressive range compression to $[0, 0.023]$ removes the adaptive signal when class complexity variation is limited. This asymmetry suggests that \iast{}'s scaling function should be conditioned on class count, not just IPC---a promising direction for future work.

Third, \emph{the class axis is the primary axis of variation}: \tags{} does not improve over \cags{}-alone on 100-class IPC=10 ($20.31\%$ vs.\ $21.11\%$), indicating that temporal modulation provides no additional benefit once per-class adaptation is applied. This finding, while negative for \tags{}, is scientifically informative: it confirms that the dominant source of suboptimality in fixed-guidance dataset distillation is the per-class guidance mismatch, not the temporal profile of guidance across timesteps.

\ags{} occupies a unique position in the landscape of diffusion-based dataset distillation: unlike LGD, which requires multiple training rounds for iterative sample selection, \ags{} preserves the training-free, single-pass paradigm of MGD$^3$ while delivering consistent improvements through principled guidance adaptation. The framework is orthogonal to sample selection methods and could be combined with them in future work. Additionally, the multi-factor complexity metric and adaptive guidance schedules may transfer to other generative tasks beyond dataset distillation, such as data augmentation and synthetic data generation for domain adaptation. Future work will explore learning the complexity weights and schedule parameters from data, conditioning \iast{}'s scaling on class count, extending the framework to text-to-image diffusion models, and investigating the interaction between \ags{} and other guidance techniques such as self-attention guidance~\cite{hong2022improving}.
