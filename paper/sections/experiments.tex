\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on ImageNet-100 (both 10-class and 100-class subsets)~\cite{russakovsky2014imagenet}, ImageNette, ImageWoof, a 200-class ImageNet subset, and the full ImageNet-1K (1000 classes). To test generalization beyond ImageNet, we evaluate on Food-101~\cite{bossard2014food101} (101 food categories) and CIFAR-10~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning} (10 object categories) using Stable Diffusion~\cite{rombach2021highresolution} 1.5 as the generator. All images are resized to $256 \times 256$ pixels.

\paragraph{Architecture and protocol.}
Following MGD$^3$~\cite{chansantiago2025modeguided}, we use a pretrained DiT-XL/2~\cite{peebles2022scalable} as the frozen generator for all ImageNet-derived experiments, with 50 denoising steps and a high-noise guidance window covering the first 25 timesteps. We evaluate downstream classification using ConvNet-6, ResNet-18, and ResNetAP-10~\cite{kaiming2015deep}, all with instance normalization. Classifiers are trained with SGD (lr $0.1$, weight decay $1\text{e}{-4}$, batch size $128$) with a multi-step LR schedule. We use 2000 epochs for 100-class experiments, ImageNette, and ImageWoof; 3000 for the 10-class subset and CIFAR-10; 1000 for IN-1K. Data augmentation includes random resized crop, horizontal flip, color jitter, PCA lighting noise, and CutMix. Following MGD$^3$, we report the \emph{best} top-1 accuracy across all training epochs. All main 100-class IPC=10 experiments use five random seeds; all other experiments use three, and we report mean $\pm$ standard deviation (population std for reporting; sample std for $t$-tests).

\paragraph{\ags{} configurations.}
For \cags{}, we use the complexity weighting $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta) = (0, 0.5, 0.5, 0)$---the optimal configuration identified by our factor ablation (\Cref{sec:factor-ablation}) and weight grid search (Appendix~\ref{sec:weight-learning})---with sigmoid parameters $s = 3.0$, $c_0 = 0.6$, and guidance range $[0.0, 0.06]$.

\subsection{Main Results: ImageNet-100 (100-class)}
\label{sec:results-100cls}

\Cref{tab:sota-main} presents our main results on ImageNet-100 (100-class, IPC=10) alongside published baselines from the MGD$^3$ paper~\cite{chansantiago2025modeguided}. The unguided baseline achieves $22.90\%$ on ConvNet-6, $26.52\%$ on ResNet-18, and $26.51\%$ on ResNetAP-10. Fixed guidance at $\lambda = 0.10$ yields $21.31 \pm 0.42\%$ on ConvNet-6 ($-6.5\%$ relative), confirming that globally fixed guidance is suboptimal. \cags{} achieves $25.55 \pm 0.21\%$, $30.81 \pm 0.43\%$, and $29.74 \pm 0.46\%$ on the three architectures. Critically, \cags{} surpasses all published baselines on all three architectures: on ResNet-18, $30.81\%$ vs.\ MGD$^3$'s $23.6\%$ ($+7.21\%$) and IDC-1's $25.1\%$ ($+5.71\%$); on ResNetAP-10, $29.74\%$ vs.\ $25.8\%$ ($+3.94\%$); on ConvNet-6, $25.55\%$ vs.\ $23.4\%$ ($+2.15\%$) and $24.3\%$ ($+1.25\%$). The stronger gains on deeper architectures (ResNet-18: $+16.2\%$ relative vs.\ ConvNet-6: $+11.6\%$) indicate that \cags{}-generated images contain richer discriminative features better exploited by more expressive classifiers. On the 10-class subset, \cags{} is neutral ($-0.7\%$ relative at IPC=10), confirming that per-class adaptation provides no benefit when class complexity variation is limited (Appendix~\ref{sec:results-10cls}).

\subsection{Complexity Factor Ablation}
\label{sec:factor-ablation}

To understand the contribution of each complexity factor, we conduct a factor ablation on ImageNet-100 (100-class, IPC=10, ConvNet-6, 3 seeds) testing single-factor variants, equal weights, and variance-dominated weights (\Cref{tab:factor-ablation}). All factor variants improve over unguided ($22.79\%$ from the 3-seed evaluation), confirming that any form of per-class adaptive guidance is beneficial. The ranking reveals two key patterns. First, cluster entropy alone achieves the highest mean accuracy ($25.31 \pm 0.62\%$), but with the highest seed variance. Second, inter-class separability alone is the weakest ($23.75 \pm 0.09\%$), producing near-constant per-class guidance (std $0.006$)---effectively a fixed guidance at $\lambda \approx 0.04$. Crucially, all three configurations that reduce or eliminate separability weight outperform the full four-factor design ($24.24\%$), while the separability-dominated configuration matches it. This provides strong evidence that separability is the least effective factor and that the degree of per-class guidance variation---not the number of factors---is the key driver of \cags{}'s improvement.

The two-factor optimal $(0, 0.5, 0.5, 0)$ resolves this by concentrating all weight on entropy and intra-class variance---the two factors with the widest per-class spread---maximizing the range of guidance strengths. This widens the per-class $\lambda$ variation by $2.3\times$ (std $0.0049$ vs.\ $0.0021$ for the four-factor design, computed from the cached complexity scores), directly translating to larger accuracy gains. A class-level mechanism analysis (\Cref{fig:class-level-mechanism}, Appendix~\ref{sec:class-analysis}) confirms that higher-complexity quartiles receive stronger guidance and exhibit larger gains ($2.8\times$ difference between top and bottom halves).

\subsection{Statistical Significance}
\label{sec:significance}

Using five seeds for the main 100-class IPC=10 experiments and three for others, the improvements on the 100-class subset are statistically significant ($p < 0.05$) at all IPC settings, with large effect sizes (Cohen's $d \geq 3.90$). Cross-architecture results with 5 seeds are highly significant ($p < 0.001$ for all three architectures, Cohen's $d \geq 6.2$). The neutral 10-class IPC=10 result is confirmed non-significant ($p = 0.721$).

\subsection{D4M Comparison and 2026 Methods}
\label{sec:d4m-summary}

We implement D4M~\cite{su2024dataset} under our exact protocol. At $t_{\text{start}}{=}40$ (its optimum), D4M achieves $23.07\%$---falling short of \cags{} and barely exceeding unguided ($22.90\%$). Full D4M sensitivity analysis, cross-dataset comparison, and comparison with 2026 methods (DMGD~\cite{wang2026dmgd}, LGD~\cite{chansantiago2026learnabilityguided}) are in Appendix~\ref{sec:dd-baselines}. A visual summary of results across architectures and class counts is in \Cref{fig:results-comparison} (Appendix~\ref{sec:results-figure}). DMGD achieves higher absolute accuracy on ImageNette/ImageWoof, but with a $5.1\%$ baseline advantage due to lighter augmentation; in relative improvement, \cags{} ($+14.8\%$) is comparable to DMGD ($+15.7\%$) while adding negligible overhead.

\subsection{CAGS+D4M Hybrid Ablation}
\label{sec:hybrid-ablation}

To test whether D4M's real-image initialization complements \cags{}'s adaptive guidance, we construct a hybrid pipeline (VAE encode $\to$ add noise at $t_{\text{start}} \to$ denoise with per-class \cags{} guidance), sweeping $t_{\text{start}} \in \{35, 40, 45\}$ on ConvNet-6. The hybrid peaks at $t_{\text{start}}{=}45$: $25.17 \pm 0.37\%$, and is evaluated on all three architectures (\Cref{tab:sota-main}). The hybrid does \emph{not} improve over \cags{}-alone on any architecture: ConvNet-6 $25.17\%$ vs.\ $25.55\%$ ($-0.38\%$), ResNet-18 $30.18\%$ vs.\ $30.81\%$ ($-0.63\%$), and ResNetAP-10 $29.51\%$ vs.\ $29.74\%$ ($-0.23\%$, but with $2.75\times$ higher variance). This reinforces the core message: \cags{}'s training-free, pure-noise approach is both simpler and more effective than combining real-image initialization with adaptive guidance.
