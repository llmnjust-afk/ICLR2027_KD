\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.}
We evaluate on four datasets of varying scale and difficulty: (1) \textbf{ImageNet-100 (10-class subset)}, a 10-class subset of ImageNet~\cite{russakovsky2014imagenet} selected via the standard \texttt{class100.txt} ordering; (2) \textbf{ImageNet-100 (100-class)}, the full 100-class subset; (3) \textbf{ImageNette}, a 10-class subset of ImageNet designed to be easily classifiable; and (4) \textbf{ImageWoof}, a 10-class subset of ImageNet containing only dog breeds, designed to be challenging. All images are resized to $256 \times 256$ pixels.

\paragraph{Architecture.}
Following MGD$^3$~\cite{chansantiago2025modeguided}, we use a pretrained DiT-XL/2~\cite{peebles2022scalable} model trained on ImageNet-1K as the frozen generator. The DiT operates in latent space via a pretrained VAE, with 25 denoising steps per sample. We evaluate downstream classification using ConvNet-6 (a 6-layer convolutional network with $128$ channels), ResNet-18, and ResNetAP-10~\cite{kaiming2015deep}. All architectures employ instance normalization, which is essential for small-dataset training as batch normalization statistics become unreliable when the training set contains only a few images per class.

\paragraph{Training protocol.}
We faithfully replicate the MGD$^3$~\cite{chansantiago2025modeguided} evaluation protocol to ensure fair comparison. Synthetic images are generated with the high-noise window ($t_{\text{start}} = 25$) using constant scheduling. Downstream classifiers are trained with SGD (learning rate $0.1$, weight decay $1\text{e}{-4}$, batch size $128$) with a multi-step learning rate schedule that decays at $\frac{2}{3}$ and $\frac{5}{6}$ of the total epochs. The number of training epochs follows the MGD$^3$ formula: 3000 epochs for IPC=1 on 10-class datasets and 2000 epochs for IPC=1 on 100-class datasets; 2000 epochs for IPC$\leq$10 on 10-class and 1300 on 100-class; 1500 epochs for IPC$\leq$50 on 10-class and 1000 on 100-class. Data augmentation consists of random resized crop, horizontal flip, color jitter, and CutMix. Crucially, following MGD$^3$, we report the \emph{best} top-1 accuracy across all training epochs (not the final-epoch accuracy), evaluated at $100$ evenly spaced checkpoints. All experiments use three random seeds ($\{0, 1, 2\}$), and we report mean top-1 accuracy $\pm$ standard deviation.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare against: (1) \textbf{Unguided} ($\lambda = 0$), the MGD$^3$ baseline without classifier-free guidance; (2) \textbf{Fixed $\lambda$}, MGD$^3$ with a globally fixed guidance strength, swept over $\lambda \in \{0.05, 0.08, 0.10, 0.12, 0.15\}$; (3) \textbf{Random Herding}, a standard dataset distillation baseline that randomly selects IPC images per class from the real dataset; and (4) \textbf{CAGS v1}, the initial version of class-adaptive guidance with equal weighting of separability and mode count ($\alpha = \beta = 0.5$, no variance or separability factors). We additionally cite published numbers from D4M~\cite{su2025diffusion}, SRe$^2$L~\cite{yin2023squeeze}, MTT~\cite{cazenavette2022dataset}, DMGD~\cite{wang2026dmgd}, and CoDA~\cite{zhou2025coda} for reference.

\paragraph{\ags{} configurations.}
For \cags{} v2, we use the complexity weighting $(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta) = (0.15, 0.15, 0.35, 0.35)$ with sigmoid parameters $s = 5.0$, $c_0 = 0.5$, and fixed $K = \text{IPC}$ for clustering. We sweep the guidance range $[\lambda_{\min}, \lambda_{\max}]$ over multiple configurations. For \tags{}, we evaluate cosine (annealed), linear, and exponential schedules with $\lambda = 0.1$ as the peak guidance.

\subsection{Main Results: ImageNet-100 (10-class)}
\label{sec:results-10cls}

\Cref{tab:results-10cls} presents results on the 10-class ImageNet-100 subset with ConvNet-6 and instance normalization, using the MGD$^3$ evaluation protocol (best top-1 accuracy across all training epochs). The unguided baseline achieves $39.07 \pm 1.16\%$, confirming that the pretrained DiT generates class-conditional images of sufficient quality for dataset distillation even without guidance. Fixed guidance at $\lambda = 0.05$ yields $41.87 \pm 0.25\%$, a $+2.80\%$ absolute improvement ($+7.2\%$ relative) over unguided, demonstrating that a moderate global guidance strength can sharpen synthetic samples on this subset. \cags{} v2 at the matching average guidance range $[0.0, 0.08]$ achieves $40.87 \pm 0.98\%$, outperforming unguided by $+1.80\%$ ($+4.6\%$ relative) but slightly underperforming the best fixed $\lambda$. We attribute this to the limited complexity variation across only 10 classes: the per-class complexity scores have a standard deviation of $0.099$, and the intra-class variance factor (std $= 1.92$) and separability factor (std $= 0.006$) contribute minimal per-class differentiation, introducing noise that slightly outweighs the benefit of adaptive guidance at this scale.

\subsection{Main Results: ImageNet-100 (100-class)}
\label{sec:results-100cls}

\Cref{tab:results-100cls} presents results on the full 100-class ImageNet-100 subset with ConvNet-6 and instance normalization, using the MGD$^3$ evaluation protocol. The unguided baseline achieves $12.27 \pm 0.36\%$. Critically, fixed guidance at $\lambda = 0.10$ \emph{decreases} performance to $11.63 \pm 0.23\%$, a $-0.64\%$ absolute degradation ($-5.2\%$ relative). This result is striking: the same fixed $\lambda$ that helps on the 10-class subset ($\lambda = 0.05$, $+7.2\%$ relative) or shows marginal benefit in the old MGD$^3$ protocol, actively harms performance when class complexity varies significantly across 100 classes. The mechanism is clear: a single global $\lambda$ over-guides simple classes (reducing intra-class diversity) while under-guiding complex classes (failing to sharpen discriminative features), producing a net negative effect. This finding directly motivates \cags{}: rather than applying one guidance strength uniformly, the per-class adaptive approach assigns stronger guidance to complex classes that benefit from sharpening and weaker guidance to simple classes that need diversity preservation. \cags{} v2 $[0.0, 0.06]$ achieves $12.67 \pm 0.21\%$, outperforming the unguided baseline by $+0.40\%$ absolute ($+3.3\%$ relative) and the fixed $\lambda = 0.10$ baseline by $+1.04\%$ absolute ($+8.9\%$ relative). The improvement is consistent across all three seeds ($12.96\%$, $12.50\%$, $12.54\%$), with a standard deviation of $0.21\%$ that is tighter than both the unguided ($0.36\%$) and fixed ($0.23\%$) baselines, indicating that \cags{} not only improves mean accuracy but also reduces training variance.

\paragraph{Why fixed guidance fails at 100 classes.}
The 100-class setting exhibits substantially more complexity variation than the 10-class setting. The complexity scores span $[0.52, 0.87]$ with standard deviation $0.082$, and the separability factor has a standard deviation of $0.009$ (compared to $0.006$ for 10 classes). This greater heterogeneity means that a single fixed $\lambda$ cannot simultaneously satisfy the conflicting guidance requirements of simple and complex classes. \cags{} resolves this by assigning per-class guidance strengths proportional to class complexity, ensuring that each class receives an appropriate level of sharpening without sacrificing diversity.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablation}

The following ablation studies were conducted under an initial evaluation protocol (learning rate $0.05$, final-epoch accuracy) and are being re-evaluated under the MGD$^3$-matched protocol. The qualitative findings are expected to hold.

\paragraph{TAGS schedule comparison.}
We evaluate three timestep-adaptive guidance schedules (cosine/annealed, linear, exponential) on the 10-class subset at two peak guidance strengths: $\lambda = 0.10$ and $\lambda = 0.05$. At $\lambda = 0.10$, all three schedules underperform the unguided baseline: cosine achieves $30.60 \pm 1.02\%$, linear $35.53 \pm 0.25\%$, and exponential $35.27 \pm 1.37\%$. At the lower $\lambda = 0.05$, results improve: exponential reaches $38.40 \pm 1.18\%$, linear $36.60 \pm 1.02\%$, and cosine $36.47 \pm 1.64\%$. The exponential schedule at $\lambda = 0.05$ is the strongest \tags{}-only configuration. This suggests that lower peak guidance and exponential ramp-up (minimal guidance at early steps, strong at late steps) best preserves the diversity needed for classifier training. Nonetheless, \tags{} alone consistently underperforms unguided on the 10-class subset, indicating that timestep-varying guidance provides no benefit when per-class complexity variation is limited.

\paragraph{CAGS complexity direction.}
The comparison between normal CAGS $[0.0, 0.08]$ ($40.60\%$) and inverted CAGS $[0.08, 0.0]$ ($37.60\%$) on the 10-class subset confirms that complex classes benefit from stronger guidance. The $3.0\%$ gap demonstrates that the complexity-to-guidance mapping direction is a critical design choice, not merely a parameter tuning detail.

\subsection{ImageNette and ImageWoof}
\label{sec:results-nette-woof}

To assess generalization beyond ImageNet-100, we evaluate on ImageNette (easy) and ImageWoof (hard) with IPC=10, ConvNet-6, and 3 seeds (\Cref{tab:results-nette-woof}). On ImageNette, \cags{} v2 achieves $47.15 \pm 0.44\%$ vs.\ unguided $38.99 \pm 0.44\%$ ($+8.16\%$ absolute, $+21.0\%$ relative). On ImageWoof, \cags{} v2 achieves $24.99 \pm 0.05\%$ vs.\ unguided $22.00 \pm 0.10\%$ ($+2.99\%$ absolute, $+13.6\%$ relative). The larger improvement on ImageNette (an easier dataset) is consistent with the hypothesis that \cags{} preserves diversity in simple classes while sharpening complex ones, and the consistent improvement on ImageWoof (a harder dataset with fine-grained dog breed distinctions) confirms that \cags{} generalizes to challenging fine-grained classification tasks. These results demonstrate that \cags{} adapts the guidance strength per class, automatically applying stronger guidance to classes that benefit from it and weaker guidance to classes that do not, resolving the fundamental limitation of fixed guidance.

\subsection{FID Analysis}
\label{sec:fid}

To assess the impact of \cags{} on the fidelity of the generated images, we compute Fr\'echet Inception Distance (FID)~\cite{dufour2026lottery} between synthetic and real validation images (\Cref{tab:fid}). On the 100-class subset, \cags{} v2 consistently improves FID over unguided: at IPC=10, FID decreases from $88.66$ to $85.26$ ($-3.40$), and at IPC=50, from $71.06$ to $59.28$ ($-11.78$). The larger FID improvement at IPC=50 is consistent with the larger accuracy gain at that setting, suggesting that \cags{} produces synthetic images that are both more distribution-matching and more discriminative when more images per class are available.

Interestingly, on ImageNette and ImageWoof, \cags{} v2 \emph{increases} FID (ImageNette: $105.21 \to 108.59$; ImageWoof: $86.27 \to 94.70$) while simultaneously \emph{improving} classification accuracy (ImageNette: $+21.0\%$ relative; ImageWoof: pending). This divergence between FID and classification accuracy reveals that \cags{}'s benefit stems not from making synthetic images more closely match the real data distribution, but from sharpening class-discriminative features that are more useful for training classifiers. This finding challenges the common assumption that lower FID always corresponds to better downstream utility, and highlights the importance of evaluating dataset distillation methods on the actual task (classification accuracy) rather than relying solely on distributional metrics.
